Royco · Dawn Market Design

Plain-language legend

Every term in the simulator, explained as simply as possible. No finance background needed. If a word on the screen looks technical, it's explained here.

Start hereThe one big idea

Tranching (splitting risk)

Take one investment and split it into two slices with different risk. One slice is made safe, the other takes the hits first but is paid extra for it. That's the whole product. In this tool the safe slice is the Senior and the risky slice is the Junior.

Base strategy

The underlying investment being split, before any slicing. Its value goes up and down over time. Everything the Senior and Junior earn or lose comes from this one line.

Here the base strategy is a Curve liquidity-pool position: its value in dollars, day by day.

The two slicesSenior & Junior

Senior tranche the safe side

The protected slice. It only loses money after the Junior's cushion is completely used up. In exchange for that safety it earns a steadier, usually lower return. Think of it as the senior lender who gets paid first.

Junior tranche the risky side

The first-loss slice. If the base strategy drops, the Junior absorbs the loss first to shield the Senior. In return it earns extra yield. It can do very well or get wiped out. Think of it as the investor who takes the risk for a bigger reward.

First-loss cushion / coverage a % you set

How big the Junior's protective buffer is, as a share of the market. A 40% cushion means the base strategy can fall about 40% before the Senior feels anything: the Junior eats that first. Bigger cushion = safer Senior, but a thinner Junior that swings more.

The dialsTerms you choose

First-loss coverage (%)

The size of the Junior cushion above. Higher = Senior better protected.

Observation period (days)

When the Junior falls behind, it gets a grace window to recover before the loss is locked in. Longer window = more chances for the Junior to bounce back, but the Senior waits longer to be sure it's safe.

Yield share to Junior (%)

What slice of the Senior's yield is handed to the Junior as its reward for taking the first losses. Higher = Junior earns more (and is more attractive to fill), Senior keeps less.

Senior exit buffer

A safety trip-wire: if the Junior cushion gets dangerously thin, the Senior is allowed to exit early to protect itself. The buffer is how much cushion must remain before that exit unlocks.

Self-liquidation bonus

A small extra reward paid to Seniors who actually take that early exit, to encourage an orderly unwind.

The yieldWhere the return comes from

Yield

The income the investment throws off over time (fees, rewards), separate from whether its price goes up or down.

Amplified yield (Convex / StakeDAO)

Convex and StakeDAO are services that sit on top of a Curve position and boost its yield with extra reward tokens: without changing the price risk. So the Junior can earn a much fatter return for taking the same drawdown. In this market that boost is the bulk of the yield.

APY

Annual Percentage Yield: the yield expressed as a per-year rate. 28% APY ≈ earning 28% over a year if it held.

The history chartWhat the lines mean

Senior share price & Junior share price

What $100 put into each slice would be worth over time, given the historical ups and downs. The Senior line stays smoother; the Junior line swings much more.

Base strategy line

The underlying value before splitting: the raw material both slices are made from.

Observation period (shaded band)

A stretch where the Junior was behind and inside its grace window, trying to recover.

Junior recovery erased

The grace window ran out before the Junior fully recovered, so that pending recovery was written off. It's a realized cost to the Junior.

Senior loss event the red flag

A moment where losses were so deep the Junior cushion was fully used and the Senior itself took a hit. For the product to be sound, this should ideally never happen.

The scorecardOutcome metrics

Drawdown / worst drop

The biggest fall from a peak to a later low. "Junior worst drop −77%" means at some point the Junior was down 77% from its high.

Claims erased / claims value erased

How many Junior recoveries were written off (see "recovery erased"), and their total dollar value.

Senior loss events

How many times the Senior was hit. 0 is the goal.

avg/yr & total (CAGR)

The return expressed as an average per year, and over the whole period. Over very short histories the per-year number can look extreme: read it with the total.

The crystal ballMonte Carlo & GO/NO-GO

Monte Carlo

Instead of guessing one future, the tool rolls the dice thousands of times to draw thousands of possible futures, then looks at the whole spread of outcomes. It answers "what usually happens, and how bad can the bad cases get?"

Price volatility

How jumpy the price is, per year. Higher volatility = wider swings = more chance of a deep drawdown. A calm market might be 20%; a wild one 60%+.

Normalized (ex-tails) vs Full history

Two ways to set that volatility. Normalized strips out the handful of extreme "black-swan" days to estimate a calmer, everyday level. Full history keeps everything, including the crash. Use normalized if you think the crash was a one-off.

Price drift

Your assumption for the price's average direction over the horizon (yield aside). 0% = sideways (goes nowhere on average, just wiggles). Positive = you expect it to rise; negative = to keep falling.

Horizon

How far into the future the simulation looks, in days. 365 = one year of possible futures.

Fan chart & 10-90% band

The shaded fan shows the range of outcomes: the middle line is the typical (median) path, and the band covers the middle 80% of futures (10th to 90th). A wide band = very uncertain.

P(Senior loss)

The chance, across all those simulated futures, that the Senior takes any loss. Lower is better: the launch rule wants this under 5%.

E[Junior] / Junior mean

The average ending value of a $100 Junior across all futures. Above $100 means it's expected to make money.

GO / NO-GO

The bottom-line verdict on whether it's worth launching. GO means it clears all the launch checks (Senior stays safe, Junior is expected to profit). NO-GO means it fails at least one.

Coverage scenarios table

Runs the same simulation at several cushion sizes at once, so you can see the smallest cushion that turns the market into a GO.

Illustrative model for market design, not investment advice. Nothing here is a promise of returns.
