What a tranche is, in four lines.

  • One pool, two claims on it. The Senior takes a protected, predictable rate. The Junior takes the first losses and is paid more for it.
  • The Junior is the buffer. When the pool falls, the Junior absorbs it. The Senior only loses once that buffer is used up.
  • Why a protocol wants this: the Senior is a product you can sell to capital that will not take drawdown risk, which is most capital. The Junior is sold to capital that will.
  • The only real question is how deep the buffer has to be. That is what this page is for.

Senior, flat at every coverageJunior, falls as the buffer thickens
  • Why the Senior rate never moves. Every dollar of Senior capital hands the Junior the same slice of its own yield, whatever the coverage. So the Senior always keeps the rest, and its rate is flat all the way across.
  • Why the Junior rate climbs as coverage thins. Fewer Junior dollars share that same slice. At 5% coverage each Junior dollar stands behind of Senior capital; at 50% it stands behind . Same transfer, much smaller base.
  • Coverage is a minimum, and the Junior is sized a little above it. Coverage is what the Junior must be able to absorb of Senior losses at all times. Royco's markets hold a slight excess of Junior capital over that minimum, targeting 90% utilization, so Juniors can keep entering and exiting without instantly breaching it. A 20% minimum coverage means a Junior worth 22.2% of the market.
  • The formulas. Senior APR = pool yield x (1 minus yield share). Junior APR = pool yield x (1 plus yield share x Senior size / Junior size), with Junior size = coverage / 90% target utilization. Simple annual rates, not compounded.
